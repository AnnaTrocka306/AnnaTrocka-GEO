Hospitality · GEO for hotels

When is your hotel genuinely the right recommendation?

Hotel GEO connects a property’s real characteristics and guest experience with the situations in which travellers need a suitable hotel.

A hotel is more than its stars, images and list of amenities.

A couple might be looking for peace and private spa changing facilities. A family might need spacious rooms and suitable activities for children. Other guests may seek a specific wellness experience or a quiet retreat.

To make a useful recommendation, these individual expectations must match the hotel’s real, verifiable features. Generic marketing adjectives do not achieve that.

How I investigate a hotel

Digital expectations

Which features are described publicly, and which associations appear in AI responses?

Real Guest Experience

Which attributes can I observe personally as a guest and document reliably?

Recommendation Gap

Where do the actual experience, available information and potential recommendations diverge?

What happens next

These findings inform a strategic knowledge architecture: a clear hotel identity, relevant guest situations, verifiable differentiators, sources and a semantic development path. Appropriate outcomes may include editorial content, a machine-readable knowledge base, consulting and team training.

Automation becomes useful only when the underlying information is accurate and publication rights have been verified. Otherwise it merely distributes inaccurate claims faster.

“Not every beautiful hotel is right for every guest. A good recommendation needs to understand the difference.”

Areas of work

  • GEO Gap Analysis: strategic analysis of the starting position and missing knowledge.
  • Real Guest Experience: first-hand observations with clear limits on what they demonstrate.
  • Digital knowledge architecture: consistent, reusable information about a property.
  • Consulting and training: support for hotel management and marketing teams.
  • Preparation for content automation: only after substantive groundwork and editorial checks.
This approach draws on my original GEO methodology and the independent Waldorf Astoria Dubai GEO audit (English, in progress). Any increase in hotel bookings is not an automatically demonstrated result.
