# How to Prepare Your Hotel for Future AI Recommendations

**German version:** [LINK TO GERMAN VERSION]

The way people search for, compare and choose hotels is changing quickly.

Travel agencies, comparison platforms, Google, social media and hotel websites will remain important. At the same time, another decision path is emerging: more travellers are using AI to support travel planning and hotel selection.

Current data from **[Phocuswright](https://www.phocuswright.com/Travel-Research/Research-Updates/2026/ai-isnt-just-for-research-anymore-what-european-travelers-are-actually-doing-with-it)** shows how relevant this development already is. Among AI-using travellers in Germany, hotel research is one of the most frequent applications at 39%. In the same research, 88% said AI had already influenced an actual travel decision. In another **[Phocuswright study](https://www.phocuswright.com/Travel-Research/Research-Updates/2026/the-ai-sure-hits-its-stride-5-takeaways-for-travel-leaders)**, 40% of surveyed US travellers said they would be willing to let an AI assistant book flights and hotels for them.

The next stage is already beginning.

AI systems are moving beyond research support and closer to selection and booking. In August 2026, Google introduced **[hotel booking capabilities in AI Mode](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/book-travel-ai-mode/)**, allowing users to describe their preferences, compare hotels and continue the booking process from the same AI-supported environment.

For hotels, this is not simply another marketing channel.

**The path through which a booking decision is made is changing.**

The important question is therefore no longer only:

> **How does a guest find my hotel?**

Increasingly, another question matters:

> **What does an AI system know about my hotel — and for what concrete reason could it consider or recommend this hotel for a particular guest and travel situation?**

This is exactly the change my work is designed to address.

## Why Existing Marketing Does Not Cover the Entire Task

An internal marketing team knows its hotel extremely well.

But that closeness can also create a natural blind spot. Services, processes or small details that are part of everyday hotel operations can eventually appear ordinary to the people working there.

For a guest, those same details may be an important reason to choose one hotel over another.

An external marketing or digital agency can professionally manage websites, campaigns, social media and data. But when the work is conducted mainly from a distance, this does not automatically reproduce the perspective of a guest who personally selects the hotel, stays there and pays for the experience.

In my approach, I therefore connect three levels:

> **What does the hotel actually offer?**

> **Which of those things has real decision value for a paying guest?**

> **Which of those relationships are clearly represented digitally today — and which become visible in AI recommendation situations?**

I also bring around **15 years of experience as a regularly self-paying guest in the luxury hotel segment**.

I do not know luxury hotels only from presentations, briefings or professional visits. I select them myself, book them, pay for them and compare from a customer's perspective which property is the better choice for a particular journey.

My approach therefore does not replace an internal marketing department or an existing agency.

**It adds another layer: the connection between real Guest Experience, Recommendation Strategy and the way a hotel can be represented in AI-supported decision situations.**

## What a Cooperation Can Look Like

The exact scope depends on the hotel and its objectives.

A possible process can look like this.

### Step 1: Define Clear Goals Before the Stay

The first step is to establish **what should actually be achieved**.

I review the hotel's current positioning:

- Which services are already communicated?
- Which guests are currently addressed?
- In which situations should the hotel become particularly relevant?

The central question is:

> **For which guests, and in which concrete situations, should this hotel be a particularly suitable choice?**

At the same time, we define which services and areas should receive particular attention during the stay. Existing quality or service checklists can also be included.

This creates a clear starting point before the on-site experience begins.

### Step 2: Experience the Hotel as a Real Guest

On site, I do not want to simply inspect the hotel.

I want to **experience it as a normal guest would** — from arrival and check-in to the room, restaurants, spa and the actual service throughout the stay.

Relevant observations are documented with photographs, short videos and notes.

I pay particular attention to **Wow Moments**: major or minor details that may have become normal for the hotel but can make a real difference to a guest.

Negative experiences are documented as well. They are not intended for public criticism. They are shared confidentially with the hotel as potential opportunities for improvement.

The result is documented First-Hand Knowledge about:

- what actually works;
- what creates real decision value;
- and which hotel strengths only become visible through the lived experience.

### Step 3: Turn Hotel Strengths into Concrete Recommendation Situations

The next step is to connect those real hotel strengths with situations in which people actually search for a suitable hotel.

For example, a guest might ask ChatGPT:

> **“I am going to Paris with my wife for a weekend to celebrate her birthday. Find me a suitable hotel.”**

The situation is clear:

**Paris → couple → weekend → birthday**

A hotel may already offer champagne in the room, a birthday surprise, dinner, spa access or late check-out.

This can create a strategic opportunity: those existing services could be combined and positioned as a Birthday or Romantic offer for exactly this travel situation.

The relationship becomes explicit:

**Hotel → suitable services → concrete travel situation → reason to choose**

The purpose is not to invent as many new packages as possible.

I look for situations **for which the existing hotel product is already particularly suitable**.

Whether that later becomes a new package, partner offer, campaign or another sales model remains the hotel's decision.

### Step 4: Build a Machine-Readable Knowledge Base

The verified information, observations, images, videos and Recommendation Situations are then transformed into the central knowledge layer of the system:

**a structured, machine-readable Consumer Knowledge Base.**

This is not traditional advertising copy.

Information is organised as clearly and precisely as possible:

**Service → Attribute → Guest Need → Situation → Benefit → Recommendation Reason**

For example, a hydrotherapy service is not stored simply as:

> “Hydrotherapy available.”

The Knowledge Base can also describe:

- how it can be used;
- whether privacy is possible;
- which guest need it addresses;
- and for which guest this can create a meaningful advantage.

The result is not merely a collection of isolated facts.

It is a set of explicit semantic relationships.

This reduces ambiguity and means AI-supported systems and automated processes need to reconstruct fewer important relationships from scattered information.

The Knowledge Base can also be updated continuously. New services, changed offers and additional Recommendation Situations can be added without rebuilding the hotel's entire knowledge structure.

**Up to this point, my role focuses on Research, Analysis, Recommendation Strategy and Knowledge Architecture.**

Once the Knowledge Base is ready, the analytical foundation has been created.

From this point, operational implementation begins together with my cooperation partner **ki-GEO-Beratung**.

### Step 5: Produce Content — Long-Term for GEO, Immediate for Marketing

Once the Knowledge Base exists, content production can begin directly.

Together with ki-GEO-Beratung, validated knowledge can be transformed into suitable articles, social media content, images, videos and other formats for the relevant platforms.

The visual material created during the hotel stay can also be reused.

I explain the specific role Instagram can play within GEO — and why a large follower base is not necessary for that particular function — in my article **[What Instagram Can Do for GEO — Even Without a Large Follower Base](https://ki-geo-insider.blogspot.com/2026/09/was-instagram-fur-geo-leisten-kann-auch.html)**.

For the hotel, this creates an important **double effect**.

**Long term:** suitable content increases the amount of publicly available knowledge about the hotel, its services and the situations for which it can be relevant.

GEO is a cumulative process. Changes may become visible relatively quickly, but they may also require significantly more time and cannot be guaranteed by a specific date.

**Immediately:** the same content can also be used for Paid Social and shown directly to relevant potential guests.

The hotel therefore does not need to wait until changes in AI recommendations become visible.

**The material can already support demand and booking interest today while simultaneously contributing to longer-term Recommendation Knowledge.**

### Step 6: Automate Content and Monitoring

Once the Knowledge Base and operational content structure are established, a large part of the recurring process can become automated.

The technical and operational execution is not my role as Methodology Owner.

It is carried out within the operational system together with ki-GEO-Beratung and increasingly through the application being developed for this process.

New content can reuse already validated knowledge. Hotel services and facts therefore do not need to be researched again for every article or social media post.

The process becomes much leaner:

**Knowledge Base → Content → QA → Approval → Publication → Automated Monitoring**

Monitoring can regularly check whether the hotel appears in defined AI recommendation situations, which recommendation reasons become visible and where gaps remain.

The application can store those results as new snapshots.

I can then analyse those results and identify new **[Knowledge Gaps](https://github.com/AnnaTrocka306/AnnaTrocka-GEO/blob/main/docs/knowledge-base/Knowledge_Dictionary/Knowledge_Gap.md)**, Recommendation Gaps or strategic actions.

The roles therefore remain clear:

**Application → automated production and monitoring**

**ki-GEO-Beratung → operational implementation**

**Anna Trocka → methodology, analysis, Recommendation Strategy and evaluation of results**

This can also fundamentally change the economics of recurring content production.

If a company currently spends, for example, around **€5,000 per month** on a comparable volume of manual content production, a highly automated process built on an existing Knowledge Base could potentially cost only a fraction of that amount — for example **around €500**.

This is not a fixed price promise.

The actual cost depends on content volume, platforms and the level of Human QA required.

The example simply illustrates where the saving can come from:

> **Knowledge is built once instead of being researched and restructured again for every individual piece of content.**

**Less time.**

**Lower recurring costs.**  
Content production and monitoring can be automated to a significant degree.

**Greater independence.**  
Central knowledge about the hotel remains structured within the system instead of depending exclusively on whichever employee or external agency currently holds that knowledge.

## Conclusion

I do not believe hotels need to discard their existing marketing and start again from zero.

But I am convinced that **a new layer needs to be added**.

People will continue to search for hotels themselves.

At the same time, AI systems will increasingly participate in research, comparison and selection. The transition from AI Assistant toward AI Agent has already begun.

For hotels, it therefore becomes increasingly important to represent three things clearly:

> **For which guest is this hotel suitable?**

> **In which situation?**

> **And for what real reason should it be chosen?**

This is what I developed my GEO methodology to address:

**translating real Guest Experience and existing hotel knowledge into structured Recommendation and Knowledge Architecture, from which operational measures can be implemented systematically, automated and later evaluated.**

My role remains where I believe the greatest value of my work lies:

**developing the methodology, identifying relationships, building Recommendation Strategy and using the results to determine the next strategic decisions.**
